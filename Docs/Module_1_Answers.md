- **Sub System:** Anxiety Disorder Management and Therapy Delivery System

- **Specific Focus:** Patients with anxiety disorders who lack access to timely, personalized, biofeedback-driven therapy

---

## Problem Area (Describe the local pain point in 2-3 sentences)

India has approximately 38 million people living with anxiety disorders, yet 85-90% do not receive adequate treatment due to a severe shortage of mental health professionals (only ~0.3 psychiatrists per 100,000 population vs. the WHO-recommended 3 per 100,000). Existing therapy methods are expensive, require frequent in-person clinic visits, offer no real-time physiological feedback, and cannot dynamically adapt to a patient's momentary stress levels. This results in delayed intervention, poor treatment adherence, and compounding anxiety - particularly among urban youth and working professionals who face high-stress environments but lack affordable, technology-driven support systems.

## Study the System (PID - 5W2H Framework)

### WHAT is/are the SYMPTOM(s) (Effect) of the problem observed?

1. **Massive treatment gap:** 85-90% of individuals with mental health conditions in India do not receive the care they need [Source: Mental Health Foundation of India (mhfaindia.com); PIB, Govt. of India].
2. **Rising anxiety prevalence:** Anxiety disorders in India have increased by over 120% between 1990 and 2023 [Source: Centre for Mental Health Law and Policy (CMHLP); Global Burden of Disease Study].
3. **Non-adaptive therapy:** Current treatment protocols (CBT, medication) follow fixed schedules and do not adapt in real-time to a patient's physiological state during sessions.
4. **Post-pandemic surge:** COVID-19 caused a marked rise in anxiety and depression in both primary care and general population settings across India [Source: National Medical Journal of India (NMJI)].
5. **High dropout rates:** Patients discontinue therapy due to cost, travel burden, stigma, and lack of perceivable real-time progress.

---

### WHO is affected most by the problem? (Stakeholder Group 1)

1. **Patients with anxiety disorders** - Particularly urban youth (18-35), college students, and working professionals who face academic pressure, workplace stress, and social media-driven anxiety.
2. **Women and adolescents** - Identified as groups experiencing a disproportionately higher burden of anxiety and depressive disorders [Source: CMHLP].
3. **Patients in rural/semi-urban areas** - Over 60% of India's population resides in rural areas where mental health professionals are virtually absent [Source: psychology.town; lyfsmile.com].

---

### WHO is Causing the Problem? (Stakeholder Group 2)

1. **Systemic shortage of mental health professionals** - India has ~0.3-0.75 psychiatrists per 100,000 population against the WHO recommendation of 3 per 100,000 [Source: PIB, Govt. of India; mentis.co.in].
2. **Healthcare policy gaps** - Insufficient funding and infrastructure for mental health under the National Mental Health Programme.
3. **Social stigma** - Cultural attitudes that discourage seeking mental health treatment, leading to delayed diagnosis and care.
4. **Technology gap in therapy delivery** - Absence of affordable, technology-assisted, real-time adaptive therapy systems.

---

### WHEN (Time patterns) is the Symptom happening?

- **Chronic and ongoing** - Anxiety disorders are persistent conditions that worsen without intervention.
- **Peak during high-stress periods** - Exam seasons (March-May), work deadlines, and financial year transitions.
- **Post-pandemic acceleration** - Significant worsening since 2020, with no sign of reversal.
- **Daily triggers** - Morning commutes, workplace hours (9 AM - 7 PM), and late-night screen time are common trigger periods.
- **When symptoms are NOT occurring** - During restful sleep, vacation periods, or when patients are actively engaged in therapeutic activities (meditation, exercise, nature exposure).

---

### WHERE (Space context) is/are the symptoms observed?

- **Urban metropolitan areas** - Hyderabad, Bangalore, Delhi, Mumbai - where high-stress lifestyles are prevalent.
- **Educational institutions** - Engineering colleges, medical schools, competitive exam coaching centres.
- **Workplaces** - IT corridors, startup ecosystems, BPO/call centres.
- **NOT prominently observed** - In communities with strong social support networks, active wellness programs, or access to regular counselling.

### HOW is the process cycle observed?

**Current Therapy Delivery Process Flow:**

```
Patient Experiences Anxiety Symptoms
        |
        v
Self-Assessment / Denial Phase (weeks-months)
        |
        v
Seeks Help -> Faces Stigma / Awareness Gap
        |
        v
Finds Psychiatrist/Psychologist (long wait times: 2-8 weeks)
        |
        v
In-person Session (30-60 min, Rs.500-Rs.3000/session)
        |
        v
Fixed Protocol Therapy (CBT / Medication) - NO real-time biofeedback
        |
        v
Patient Returns Home -> No Monitoring Between Sessions
        |
        v
Symptom Recurrence -> Missed Follow-ups -> Treatment Dropout
        |
        v
Cycle Repeats / Condition Worsens
```

**Key Observation:** The current system is *reactive* (wait for crisis), *non-adaptive* (same protocol regardless of real-time state), and *disconnected* (no data between sessions).

## Write the Problem Statement (Preliminary)

Approximately 38 million Indians suffer from anxiety disorders, yet 85-90% remain untreated due to a critical shortage of mental health professionals (~0.3 psychiatrists per 100,000 vs. WHO's recommended 3 per 100,000), high therapy costs, social stigma, and the absence of real-time adaptive intervention systems. Current therapy is episodic, non-responsive to real-time physiological states, and disconnected between sessions - leading to poor adherence, delayed intervention, and a compounding feedback loop where untreated anxiety worsens over time, further discouraging patients from seeking help.

**Are these problems related to each other?**
Yes - the shortage of professionals drives up costs and wait times -> patients delay seeking help -> anxiety compounds -> when they finally seek help, the condition is more severe -> fixed-protocol therapy is less effective for severe cases -> patients drop out -> the treatment gap widens further.

**What is the Causal relationship?**
Professional shortage -> high cost and wait times -> delayed treatment -> worsened condition -> poor therapy outcomes -> dropout -> untreated population grows -> increased demand on already insufficient supply.

**Which one problem is the root cause?**
The **absence of affordable, scalable, real-time adaptive therapy delivery systems** is the core enabler of the vicious cycle. While the professional shortage is structural, a technology-assisted therapy system can bridge the gap by providing accessible, biofeedback-driven, AI-personalized anxiety management.

**Is there a feedback loop?**
Yes - a reinforcing (vicious) loop:
Untreated anxiety -> Increased Severity -> Higher Cost of treatment -> Increased Patient avoidance -> Larger Untreated population -> Higher Demand on limited professionals -> Longer Wait times -> More Untreated anxiety (loop continues).

### System Components (defined by function)

| # | Component | Function |
|---|-----------|----------|
| 1 | **Anxiety Patient** | The end-user experiencing anxiety symptoms, seeking therapy |
| 2 | **Biometric Sensor (MAX30102 + ESP32)** | Measures real-time SpO2 and pulse rate of the patient |

| 8 | **Clinician / Therapist** | Monitors patient data via dashboard, adjusts treatment parameters |
| 9 | **MQTT / WebSocket Protocol** | Real-time data transport layer connecting all components |
| 10 | **Session Database** | Stores historical biometric data, session logs, and patient progress |

## Define the System Boundary (Scope)

### What is INSIDE the system boundary (scope of study):

- Biometric sensor hardware (MAX30102 + ESP32)
- VR headset and Unity application
- Spring Boot backend server
- Local AI inference container (Ollama)
- React monitoring dashboard
- Real-time data pipeline (MQTT/WebSocket)
- Session data storage

### What is OUTSIDE the system boundary (environment):

- Pharmaceutical treatment / medication
- In-person clinical therapy sessions
- Hospital/clinic infrastructure
- Health insurance systems
- Government mental health policy
- Patient's social/family support system
- Internet Service Provider / Network infrastructure

### Inputs (from environment INTO the system):

- Patient's physiological signals (heartbeat, SpO2) [GREEN ARROW]
- Patient's willingness to engage with VR therapy [GREEN ARROW]
- Clinician's treatment parameters and preferences [GREEN ARROW]
- Pre-trained AI model weights (Llama 3) [GREEN ARROW]
- Power supply and network connectivity [GREEN ARROW]

### Outputs (from the system TO the environment):

- **Desirable:** Reduced anxiety levels, improved patient self-awareness, therapy session reports for clinicians, historical trend data [RED ARROW]
- **Undesirable (waste):** Heat generation from GPU/hardware, potential sensor inaccuracy/noise, VR-induced motion sickness risk, data privacy concerns [RED ARROW]

## Stakeholder Map

### 1. Stakeholder: **Anxiety Patients (End Users)**

**Needs:**
- Affordable, accessible anxiety management tool that does not require frequent clinic visits
- Real-time feedback on their physiological state during therapy (biofeedback)
- Immersive, calming VR environments that adapt to their stress levels
- Privacy and data security for sensitive health information
- Ability to track progress over time through session history

---

### 2. Stakeholder: **Clinicians / Therapists**

**Needs:**
- Real-time dashboard to monitor patient biometrics (SpO2, pulse) during and between sessions
- Historical session data and trends to inform treatment decisions
- Ability to customize VR therapy parameters (scene type, breathing pace, session duration)
- Alerts for abnormal biometric readings
- Integration with existing clinical workflows

---

### 3. Stakeholder: **Healthcare Institutions / Hospitals**

**Needs:**
- Scalable solution that extends their mental health service capacity without proportional increase in staff
- Cost-effective alternative to fully in-person therapy delivery
- Evidence-based outcomes data to justify investment

### 4. Stakeholder (Additional): **Caregivers / Family Members**

**Needs:**
- Visibility into patient's therapy progress (with patient consent)

## Quantitative Baseline Data

### 1. Data Point A:

**Approximately 38 million people in India live with anxiety disorders, and the prevalence has increased by over 120% between 1990 and 2023.** Despite this, 85-90% of individuals with mental health conditions do not receive adequate treatment, constituting one of the largest treatment gaps globally.

**Source:** Centre for Mental Health Law and Policy (CMHLP), Global Burden of Disease Study; Mental Health Foundation of India (mhfaindia.com); Press Information Bureau (PIB), Government of India.

---

### 2. Data Point B:

**India has only ~0.3-0.75 psychiatrists per 100,000 population and ~0.07 clinical psychologists per 100,000 population**, compared to the WHO recommendation of at least 3 psychiatrists per 100,000. This means India would need approximately **42,000+ psychiatrists** to meet the WHO standard, but currently has fewer than **10,000**.

**Source:** Press Information Bureau (PIB), Government of India; WHO Mental Health Atlas; mentis.co.in; psychology.town.

---

### 3. Data Point C (Additional):

**Meta-analyses show that VR-based exposure therapy (VRET) is as efficacious as traditional in-vivo exposure therapy** for anxiety disorders, with significant reductions in self-reported anxiety and physiological markers (heart rate, HRV). VR-based biofeedback systems have demonstrated measurable improvements in heart rate variability and subjective anxiety scores.

**Source:** Frontiers in Psychology (frontiersin.org); National Institutes of Health (NIH) - PubMed Central; Journal of Medical Internet Research (JMIR).
