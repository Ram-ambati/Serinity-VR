\section*{Select a Super System}
\textbf{Healthcare and Mental Wellness System}

\section*{Select a Specific Sub System (Problem Area)}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sub System:} Anxiety Disorder Management and Therapy Delivery System
    \item \textbf{Specific Location:} Urban India (Hyderabad / Metropolitan cities)
    \item \textbf{Specific Issue:} Inaccessible, non-adaptive, and delayed anxiety treatment
    \item \textbf{Specific Focus:} Patients with anxiety disorders who lack access to timely, personalized, biofeedback-driven therapy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Problem Area}
India has approximately 38 million people living with anxiety disorders, yet 85--90\% do not receive adequate treatment due to a severe shortage of mental health professionals (only $\sim$0.3 psychiatrists per 100,000 population vs. the WHO-recommended 3 per 100,000). Existing therapy methods are expensive, require frequent in-person clinic visits, offer no real-time physiological feedback, and cannot dynamically adapt to a patient's momentary stress levels. This results in delayed intervention, poor treatment adherence, and compounding anxiety - particularly among urban youth and working professionals who face high-stress environments but lack affordable, technology-driven support systems.

\section*{Study the System (PID - 5W2H Framework)}

\subsection*{WHAT is/are the SYMPTOM(s) (Effect) of the problem observed?}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Massive treatment gap:} 85--90\% of individuals with mental health conditions in India do not receive the care they need [Source: Mental Health Foundation of India (mhfaindia.com); PIB, Govt. of India].
    \item \textbf{Rising anxiety prevalence:} Anxiety disorders in India have increased by over 120\% between 1990 and 2023 [Source: Centre for Mental Health Law and Policy (CMHLP); Global Burden of Disease Study].
    \item \textbf{Non-adaptive therapy:} Current treatment protocols (CBT, medication) follow fixed schedules and do not adapt in real-time to a patient's physiological state during sessions.
    \item \textbf{Post-pandemic surge:} COVID-19 caused a marked rise in anxiety and depression in both primary care and general population settings across India [Source: National Medical Journal of India (NMJI)].
    \item \textbf{High dropout rates:} Patients discontinue therapy due to cost, travel burden, stigma, and lack of perceivable real-time progress.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{WHO is affected most by the problem? (Stakeholder Group 1)}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Patients with anxiety disorders} - Particularly urban youth (18--35), college students, and working professionals who face academic pressure, workplace stress, and social media-driven anxiety.
    \item \textbf{Women and adolescents} - Identified as groups experiencing a disproportionately higher burden of anxiety and depressive disorders [Source: CMHLP].
    \item \textbf{Patients in rural/semi-urban areas} - Over 60\% of India's population resides in rural areas where mental health professionals are virtually absent [Source: psychology.town; lyfsmile.com].
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{WHO is Causing the Problem? (Stakeholder Group 2)}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Systemic shortage of mental health professionals} - India has $\sim$0.3--0.75 psychiatrists per 100,000 population against the WHO recommendation of 3 per 100,000 [Source: PIB, Govt. of India; mentis.co.in].
    \item \textbf{Healthcare policy gaps} - Insufficient funding and infrastructure for mental health under the National Mental Health Programme.
    \item \textbf{Social stigma} - Cultural attitudes that discourage seeking mental health treatment, leading to delayed diagnosis and care.
    \item \textbf{Technology gap in therapy delivery} - Absence of affordable, technology-assisted, real-time adaptive therapy systems.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{WHEN (Time patterns) is the Symptom happening?}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Chronic and ongoing} - Anxiety disorders are persistent conditions that worsen without intervention.
    \item \textbf{Peak during high-stress periods} - Exam seasons (March--May), work deadlines, and financial year transitions.
    \item \textbf{Post-pandemic acceleration} - Significant worsening since 2020, with no sign of reversal.
    \item \textbf{Daily triggers} - Morning commutes, workplace hours (9 AM -- 7 PM), and late-night screen time are common trigger periods.
    \item \textbf{When symptoms are NOT occurring} - During restful sleep, vacation periods, or when patients are actively engaged in therapeutic activities (meditation, exercise, nature exposure).
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{WHERE (Space context) is/are the symptoms observed?}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Urban metropolitan areas} - Hyderabad, Bangalore, Delhi, Mumbai - where high-stress lifestyles are prevalent.
    \item \textbf{Educational institutions} - Engineering colleges, medical schools, competitive exam coaching centres.
    \item \textbf{Workplaces} - IT corridors, startup ecosystems, BPO/call centres.
    \item \textbf{NOT prominently observed} - In communities with strong social support networks, active wellness programs, or access to regular counselling.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{HOW is the process cycle observed?}
\textbf{Current Therapy Delivery Process Flow:}
\begin{verbatim}
Patient Experiences Anxiety Symptoms
        |
        v

        |
        v

        |
        v

\textbf{Key Observation:} The current system is \textit{reactive} (wait for crisis), \textit{non-adaptive} (same protocol regardless of real-time state), and \textit{disconnected} (no data between sessions).

\section*{Write the Problem Statement (Preliminary)}
Approximately 38 million Indians suffer from anxiety disorders, yet 85--90\% remain untreated due to a critical shortage of mental health professionals ($\sim$0.3 psychiatrists per 100,000 vs. WHO's recommended 3 per 100,000), high therapy costs, social stigma, and the absence of real-time adaptive intervention systems. Current therapy is episodic, non-responsive to real-time physiological states, and disconnected between sessions - leading to poor adherence, delayed intervention, and a compounding feedback loop where untreated anxiety worsens over time, further discouraging patients from seeking help.

\textbf{Are these problems related to each other?}\\
Yes - the shortage of professionals drives up costs and wait times $\rightarrow$ patients delay seeking help $\rightarrow$ anxiety compounds $\rightarrow$ when they finally seek help, the condition is more severe $\rightarrow$ fixed-protocol therapy is less effective for severe cases $\rightarrow$ patients drop out $\rightarrow$ the treatment gap widens further.

\textbf{What is the Causal relationship?}\\
Professional shortage $\rightarrow$ high cost and wait times $\rightarrow$ delayed treatment $\rightarrow$ worsened condition $\rightarrow$ poor therapy outcomes $\rightarrow$ dropout $\rightarrow$ untreated population grows $\rightarrow$ increased demand on already insufficient supply.

\textbf{Which one problem is the root cause?}\\
The \textbf{absence of affordable, scalable, real-time adaptive therapy delivery systems} is the core enabler of the vicious cycle. While the professional shortage is structural, a technology-assisted therapy system can bridge the gap by providing accessible, biofeedback-driven, AI-personalized anxiety management.

\textbf{Is there a feedback loop?}\\
Yes - a reinforcing (vicious) loop:\\
Untreated anxiety $\rightarrow$ Increased Severity $\rightarrow$ Higher Cost of treatment $\rightarrow$ Increased Patient avoidance $\rightarrow$ Larger Untreated population $\rightarrow$ Higher Demand on limited professionals $\rightarrow$ Longer Wait times $\rightarrow$ More Untreated anxiety (loop continues).

\section*{Define the System}

\subsection*{System Components (defined by function)}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Anxiety Patient:} The end-user experiencing anxiety symptoms, seeking therapy
    \item \textbf{Biometric Sensor (MAX30102 + ESP32):} Measures real-time SpO2 and pulse rate of the patient
    \item \textbf{VR Headset (Meta Quest 3):} Delivers immersive therapeutic environments (guided breathing, nature scenes)
    \item \textbf{Unity VR Application:} Renders adaptive VR scenes that respond to biometric data
    \item \textbf{Spring Boot Backend:} Central hub - routes biometric data, AI responses, and session data
    \item \textbf{Local AI Model (Ollama - Llama 3 8B):} Generates personalized therapy guidance based on biometric trends
    \item \textbf{React Dashboard:} Displays live biometric data, session history, and analytics
    \item \textbf{Clinician / Therapist:} Monitors patient data via dashboard, adjusts treatment parameters
    \item \textbf{MQTT / WebSocket Protocol:} Real-time data transport layer connecting all components
    \item \textbf{Session Database:} Stores historical biometric data, session logs, and patient progress
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Stakeholder Map}
\subsection*{1. Stakeholder: Anxiety Patients (End Users)}
\textbf{Needs:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Affordable, accessible anxiety management tool that does not require frequent clinic visits
    \item Real-time feedback on their physiological state during therapy (biofeedback)
    \item Immersive, calming VR environments that adapt to their stress levels
    \item Privacy and data security for sensitive health information
    \item Ability to track progress over time through session history
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{2. Stakeholder: Clinicians / Therapists}
\textbf{Needs:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Real-time dashboard to monitor patient biometrics (SpO2, pulse) during and between sessions
    \item Historical session data and trends to inform treatment decisions
    \item Ability to customize VR therapy parameters (scene type, breathing pace, session duration)
    \item Alerts for abnormal biometric readings
    \item Integration with existing clinical workflows
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{3. Stakeholder: Healthcare Institutions / Hospitals}
\textbf{Needs:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Scalable solution that extends their mental health service capacity without proportional increase in staff
    \item Cost-effective alternative to fully in-person therapy delivery
    \item Evidence-based outcomes data to justify investment
    \item Compliance with data privacy standards (e.g., IT Act 2000, upcoming DPDP Act)
    \item Interoperability with existing Electronic Health Record (EHR) systems
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{4. Stakeholder (Additional): Caregivers / Family Members}
\textbf{Needs:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Visibility into patient's therapy progress (with patient consent)
    \item Assurance that the system is safe and medically guided
    \item Simple, non-technical interface for understanding patient status
\end{itemize}

\section*{Quantitative Baseline Data}

\subsection*{1. Data Point A:}
\textbf{Approximately 38 million people in India live with anxiety disorders, and the prevalence has increased by over 120\% between 1990 and 2023.} Despite this, 85--90\% of individuals with mental health conditions do not receive adequate treatment, constituting one of the largest treatment gaps globally.\\
\textbf{Source:} Centre for Mental Health Law and Policy (CMHLP), Global Burden of Disease Study; Mental Health Foundation of India (mhfaindia.com); Press Information Bureau (PIB), Government of India.

\subsection*{2. Data Point B:}
\textbf{India has only $\sim$0.3--0.75 psychiatrists per 100,000 population and $\sim$0.07 clinical psychologists per 100,000 population}, compared to the WHO recommendation of at least 3 psychiatrists per 100,000. This means India would need approximately \textbf{42,000+ psychiatrists} to meet the WHO standard, but currently has fewer than \textbf{10,000}.\\
\textbf{Source:} Press Information Bureau (PIB), Government of India; WHO Mental Health Atlas; mentis.co.in; psychology.town.

\subsection*{3. Data Point C (Additional):}
\textbf{Meta-analyses show that VR-based exposure therapy (VRET) is as efficacious as traditional in-vivo exposure therapy} for anxiety disorders, with significant reductions in self-reported anxiety and physiological markers (heart rate, HRV). VR-based biofeedback systems have demonstrated measurable improvements in heart rate variability and subjective anxiety scores.\\
\textbf{Source:} Frontiers in Psychology (frontiersin.org); National Institutes of Health (NIH) - PubMed Central; Journal of Medical Internet Research (JMIR).

\section*{State "How Might We..."}

\subsection*{1. How Might We}
How might we move from a \textbf{therapy system that requires patients to physically visit scarce mental health professionals} on fixed schedules to a system that \textbf{delivers accessible, VR-based anxiety therapy at home}, using real-time biometric feedback to adapt the therapeutic experience to the patient's momentary physiological state?

\subsection*{2. How Might We}
How might we move from \textbf{static, one-size-fits-all therapy protocols} that do not account for real-time patient physiology to a system where \textbf{a local AI model dynamically personalizes therapeutic guidance} (breathing exercises, scene selection, pacing) based on live SpO2 and pulse data from wearable sensors?

\subsection*{3. How Might We}
How might we move from \textbf{disconnected, episodic therapy with no data continuity between sessions} to a system that \textbf{continuously tracks biometric trends, provides clinicians with live dashboards and historical analytics}, and enables data-driven treatment decisions - thereby improving treatment adherence and reducing the 85--90\% treatment gap?
